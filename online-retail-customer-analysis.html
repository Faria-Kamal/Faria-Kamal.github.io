Online Retail II — Repeat Purchase & Loyalty

SCENE 02 — AN AUDIT BEFORE ANSWERS

A retailer does not just want to know what it sold; it wants to know whether customers come back. This project investigates repeat-purchase behaviour in a real transaction dataset — starting with a documented data-quality audit before a single row is removed.

The business questions

This project is designed to answer five concrete questions:

  1. What share of identifiable customers purchase more than once?
  2. How long does it typically take a customer to return?
  3. How does repeat purchasing change over time?
  4. Which customer groups behave differently?
  5. What can a retailer realistically do with these findings?

The aim is a small number of defensible conclusions — not a wall of charts.

Where the audit stands

The two annual worksheets were loaded and combined into a single dataframe of 1,067,371 rows and 8 columns. The next step was to understand data quality before any cleaning: missing values, exact duplicates, negative quantities, cancellation invoices, unusual prices, date coverage and unique counts.

Status: audit code has been run. Numerical outputs are being checked and will be published here once confirmed — no findings are claimed before verification. The notebook lives in this repository ↗.

One early issue is already clear: roughly 22% of Customer ID values are missing. That matters because a missing ID means a transaction cannot be reliably linked to a person.

Proposed cleaning rules — and why

These are decisions, not yet final results. Each has a reason:

  • Keep the original dataset intact. Analysis uses separate views so no evidence is destroyed.
  • Missing Customer IDs: retained for overall transaction analysis, excluded from customer-level repeat-purchase calculations. Throwing away 22% of transactions would bias the picture.
  • Cancelled invoices (invoice numbers beginning with C): kept in the original data but excluded from completed-purchase counts. A cancellation is not a successful purchase.
  • Negative quantities: investigated as returns/credits rather than silently deleted; excluded from the main purchase dataset once confirmed.
  • Exact duplicate rows: counted and sampled first, then removable from the cleaned analysis dataset with the count documented.
  • Country: retained — it may reveal meaningful differences in repeat behaviour.
  • InvoiceDate: converted to datetime, then used to derive date, month, year and hour for time-based analysis.

Planned analysis path

  1. Finalise the data-quality report with verified counts.
  2. Build a clean "completed purchases" dataset and a separate "returns" view.
  3. Define a repeat customer explicitly, then calculate frequency and repeat rate.
  4. Measure time between purchases and examine monthly/hourly patterns.
  5. Compare customer groups and summarise limitations for a retailer audience.

The most important interview point here is definition: how a repeat customer is defined changes the answer, so it must be stated clearly rather than assumed.

Known limitations

  • Customer identity is unavailable for a substantial share of transactions.
  • The data covers a specific retailer and period; generalisation is limited.
  • Cancellations and returns need separate treatment from completed sales.
  • No results are published yet — this page documents method and progress, not outcomes.
